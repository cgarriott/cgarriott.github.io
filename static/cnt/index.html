Contested words stay in Greek.

In this translation about seven percent of the words are left untranslated. The untranslated words are those that have aroused dispute among past interpreters. The point of leaving them untranslated is to make the reader sensitive to the passages where interpreters have felt they must appeal to grammar, history, or philosophy. Many theologies depend in part on how a particular word has been rendered, and this translation shows which words those are. The untranslated words are marked by type, and each can be read in Greek, in transliteration, and in gloss.

Every untranslated word carries an endnote with references to the secondary literature. The selection of the untranslated words and the drafting of the endnotes were both done with artificial intelligence. A language model read commentaries and academic articles to locate disputes over meaning. It sorted them into three types.

The three marks

Theological: always untranslated Crux: untranslated here Variant: editions disagree
