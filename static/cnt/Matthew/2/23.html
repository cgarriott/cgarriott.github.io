CNT — Corey's Non-Translation
Theological: always untranslated Crux: untranslated here Variant: editions disagree

:

καὶ ἐλθὼν κατῴκησεν εἰς πόλιν λεγομένην Ναζαρέτ, ὅπως πληρωθῇ τὸ ῥηθὲν διὰ τῶν προφητῶν ὅτι Ναζωραῖος κληθήσεται.

And having come, he settled in a polisa called Nazareth, so that what was spoken through the prophets might reach its pleromab, that he will be called a Nazoreanc.

Notes

a
polin Nazareth was a small, obscure Galilean village, unmentioned anywhere in the Hebrew Bible. First-century population estimates run under five hundred. Translations split between "city" and "town" for polis, with no footnote in any of them addressing the choice.↑
Usually city, town
Sources
  • Hart, MacArthur Study Bible, Ignatius Bible and Orthodox Study Bible — render "city"
  • NRSVue, HCSB and The Jewish Annotated New Testament — render "town"
  • France (NICNT) — cites J. F. Strange's population estimate of under five hundred (Anchor Bible Dictionary 4:1050)
b
plērōthēi This is the fourth of ten fulfillment citations in Matthew, and the only one built this way. Every other instance names a single prophet, or says simply "the prophet," and introduces a direct quotation with "saying." Here the plural "the prophets" appears with no name, introduced by "that" rather than "saying," and no known Old Testament text says anything close to "he will be called a Nazorean."↑
Usually fulfilled, filled, full
Sources
  • Turner (BECNT) — reads the grammatical shift as his own tie-breaker for a "theme, not one text" reading
  • Mitch and Sri (CCSS) — treat the citation as pointing to a prophetic theme rather than one passage
  • Menken ("The Sources of the Old Testament Quotation in Matthew 2:23," JBL) — dissents, arguing the verse is a genuine, reworked quotation of Judges 13:5, 7
  • Mushayabasa ("Conceptual Blending in Matthew 2:23," In die Skriflig) — reframes Menken's Judges link as a "conceptual blend" of texts, but lands closer to the theme reading
c
Nazōraios No Old Testament text says "he will be called a Nazorean." Proposed derivations include the Hebrew netser, "branch," from Isaiah 11:1, and nazir, "one consecrated to God," from Judges 13, reached by way of Isaiah 4:3's "he will be called holy." Others point to "watchmen" in Jeremiah 31:6, to a "despised and rejected" theme tied to Nazareth's obscurity, or to the plain sense of a person from Nazareth with no wordplay at all. A minority position reads the verse as a genuine, reshaped quotation of Judges 13:5, 7 rather than a thematic gesture.↑
Usually nazareth, nazarene
Sources
  • Mitch and Sri (CCSS) — read it as a play on Hebrew netser, "branch" (Isaiah 11:1)
  • Brown (The Birth of the Messiah) — traces it to nazir, "one consecrated to God," by way of Isaiah 4:3's "he will be called holy"
  • Albright and Mann (Anchor) — link it instead to "watchmen" in Jeremiah 31:6
  • Keener (A Commentary on the Gospel of Matthew) — favors the netser reading and rejects the Isaiah 4:3 derivation as too indirect
  • Menken ("The Sources of the Old Testament Quotation in Matthew 2:23," JBL) — argues the verse directly quotes Judges 13:5, 7, reshaped
