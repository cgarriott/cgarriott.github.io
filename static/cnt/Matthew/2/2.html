CNT — Corey's Non-Translation
Theological: always untranslated Crux: untranslated here Variant: editions disagree

:

λέγοντες· Ποῦ ἐστιν ὁ τεχθεὶς βασιλεὺς τῶν Ἰουδαίων; εἴδομεν γὰρ αὐτοῦ τὸν ἀστέρα ἐν τῇ ἀνατολῇ καὶ ἤλθομεν προσκυνῆσαι αὐτῷ.

saying, "Where is the born king of the Jews? For we saw his star in the anatolēa, and we came to proskynieb him."

Notes

a
anatolēi ἀνατολή is the noun cognate to ἀνατέλλω, "to rise," a verb used of stars and the sun. The phrase can mean either "in the east," repeating the geographic claim already made at 2:1, or "at its rising," reporting an astronomical event. Three verses later the same phrase describes a star moving ahead of the travelers, something a fixed compass direction cannot do, favoring the astronomical reading.↑
Usually east, rising
Sources
  • Davies and Allison (ICC) — calls the phrase "generally recognized" as a technical astronomical phrase for a star's rising, citing Homer, Plato, and Euripides
  • Blass, Debrunner and Funk (A Greek Grammar of the New Testament) — the often-cited rule that compass points take no article turns out, on direct inspection, to be a single unsupported sentence with no New Testament example
  • Molnar (The Star of Bethlehem: The Legacy of the Magi) — reads the phrase as technical astrological vocabulary for a star's heliacal rising
  • Blomberg (NAC) — raises a syntactic alternative, that the phrase may modify "we saw" rather than "star"
b
proskunēsai This is the first of three uses of the verb in this pericope, repeated at 2:8 and 2:11. The construction matches ordinary court homage to a human ruler, the same one Herod uses for his own pretext three verses later. Matthew applies the identical grammatical construction elsewhere to undisputed worship of Jesus, so grammar alone cannot settle which sense is meant here.↑
Usually worship, homage, kneeling down
Sources
  • Carson (EBC), citing Broadus — "worship" need not imply the magi recognized Jesus's divinity, and may simply mean "do homage"
  • Davies and Allison (ICC) — the Septuagint regularly pairs "come" with "worship" as a single cultic action, the same construction the magi's own request uses
  • Mitch and Sri (CCSS) — reads the magi's homage as already substantively divine worship, without flagging the ambiguity other commentators debate
  • Hagner (WBC) — Matthew's readers know the fuller sense of what the magi are doing better than the magi themselves would have known
